Using several lamps

Choose how many lamps to add, then select a numbered lamp to set its recording, brightness, flicker strength and position. Each lamp keeps its own playback time. Play all recordings starts the active lamps from their current times. Restart all together returns them to the start of their recordings. To compare aligned flicker, choose the same recording and Recorded speed for each lamp before restarting.

Where their light overlaps, the lamps add illumination. A second lamp can fill a shadow cast by the first. When their flicker rises and falls at different times, the combined light can vary less than either source on its own. Wall shadows can be switched off to compare their contribution. The recordings supply the changing rhythm. Their display gain, the mean light levels and the flame shapes remain illustrative. The viewer limits the largest displayed brightness changes, and the survey textures retain some lighting from the original photographs.

Eight-legged bison · Alcove of the Lions

In the Alcove of the Lions, the bison’s overlapping legs have been interpreted as successive positions in a run. Azéma and Rivère suggested that a lamp or torch moved along the wall could bring out those positions in turn. At the same lamp position and brightness, switch between steady light and recorded flicker and see whether your sense of movement changes. Then compare the two lamp positions. You may find that one leg outline becomes easier to see while another fades. Azéma and Rivère, figure 2.

Chauvet was discovered on 18 December 1994. Radiocarbon dates place the earlier phase of human activity around 37,000–33,500 calendar years ago and a later phase around 31,000–28,000 years ago. The earlier phase is a plausible setting for the eight-legged figure. A running cow in the same alcove has a direct charcoal date. The sampled bison was a different figure in the End Chamber, so the age of this bison remains open. Chauvet chronology · Dated figures.

Horses Fresco · Hillaire Chamber

Four horse heads rise diagonally across the panel. The overlaps put the facing rhinoceroses first, then the aurochs, then the horses. Charcoal samples date the facing rhinoceroses and give the panel an early chronological anchor. The order of the figures is clearer than the age of each horse. Panel of the Horses · Dated figures.
